# C. Physics-informed / physics-guided ML for attack and anomaly detection (systematic search and title/abstract screening)

Date of search and of all citation counts: 2026-11-23.
Screening was done on abstracts only. No full texts were read. Every abstract was pulled from the OpenAlex `abstract_inverted_index` or the Semantic Scholar (S2) Graph API `abstract` field.
Citation counts come from two sources, both checked 2026-11-23: "OA" is OpenAlex `cited_by_count` and "S2" is Semantic Scholar `citationCount`. The two sources often differ, so both are given where available.
Scopus: I could not query Scopus. Every Scopus judgement below is venue-level only and was not checked in Scopus.

## (3) Answers to the key questions and the ranked shortlist

### KQ1: Same learned detector with vs without physics, and the gains reported
**Takeaway.** Several papers report a controlled or near-controlled gain from adding physics, but only a few give numbers in the abstract. The cleanest same-architecture comparisons come from ICS and grid papers (C18, C16/C17, C14, C26, C27). In the vehicle domain the evidence is C01 (PI-LSTM vs LSTM), C05 (hybrid vs CNN-only) and C10 (feature ablation).
- C01 Philip et al. 2026: PI-LSTM "achieved the strongest attack-agnostic overall detection performance with an [AUROC] of 98.33% and F1 score of 95.74%" (abstract). The LSTM-only baseline figure (93.86%) is **not in the abstract**, so it needs a full-text locator. Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISI69914.2026.11685836).
- C05 van Wyk et al.: the CNN + Kalman/chi-square hybrid "outperforms the anomaly detection and identification capabilities of both CNNs and Kalman filtering with a χ2-detector method alone" (abstract; no numbers there). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2019.2906038).
- C18 Chahine & Noura: in a "controlled USAD experiment", physics features gave PA-F1 0.7343 vs 0.6687 for raw SCADA (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/fi18060308).
- C10 Bourouis et al.: in an architecture-preserving ablation, "raw kinematics alone reduce noise-inclusive macro-F1 from 98.67% to 67.49%" (abstract; supervised, simulated VeReMi). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/vehicles8090215).
- C14 Li et al.: physics terms "reduce the mean reconstruction error by 14.6% compared with KAN-AE"; PI-KAN-AE reaches 93.74% F1 and 99.55% recall (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/TTE.2026.3756012).
- C16 Zideh & Solanki: the Kirchhoff-loss PIConvAE "provides more accurate results compared to the data-driven ConvAE model" and "requires less training time to converge" (abstract; qualitative). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/PESGM51994.2024.10761071).
- C26 Lu et al.: on a HIL testbed, PICL-Rep AUC 0.9188 vs 0.8259 for the AE, "a paired-partition gain of 0.0929 (95% CI: 0.0407–0.1450)" (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3735797).
- C27 Sheida & Farasat: hybrid AUC 0.94 vs 0.89 for the standard AE and 0.86 for OCSVM (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPEC67884.2026.11513179).
- C25 Nafees et al.: "36.4% improvement in accuracy for coordinated attack detection with contextual information" (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/SmartGridComm52983.2022.9961034).
- C15 Yang & Ye: "reduces the false alarm rate by nearly 95% compared with CNN and other baseline models" (abstract). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.1109/APEC51134.2026.11517087).
- C11 AVMON: the hybrid (physics filter + ML residual estimator) "substantially improves detection accuracy" over physics-model-based prior defenses (abstract; qualitative). Source: [doi](https://doi.org/10.14722/vehiclesec.2024.23029).

### KQ2: Physics in the loss vs as input features vs in the anomaly score
**Takeaway.** All three placements appear in the literature. **No screened abstract reports a head-to-head comparison of physics-in-loss vs physics-as-input in the same detector.** That is RQ2 exactly, and it appears to be an open gap (bounded by this search).
- **Loss / training penalty (D4-like):** C16 and C17 (Kirchhoff's law "into the loss function", abstract); C14 (battery electrochemical constraints); C21 ("embeds discrete-time circuit equations as residual penalties", abstract); C20 (temporal-differential-consistency loss); C15 ("physics-weighted loss"); C03 (PINN learns an uncertain model parameter); C23 (swing-equation constraints).
- **Input features / residual channels (D3-like):** C02 ("innovative data features concerning critical system performance and physical dynamics", abstract); C18 (control-theoretic features); C10 (position-consistency residuals); C29 (physics residuals "combined with conventional navigation features"); C19 ("physics-augmented temporal autoencoder"). C01 uses "physics-informed residuals"; the abstract does not say whether they enter as inputs, as a loss or as a score.
- **Anomaly score / decision fusion:** C19 (late fusion of AE, physics-violation energy and Mahalanobis streams); C09 (AI evaluator + physical-coherence check); C05 (CNN + chi-square detector); C26 (residual trigger fused with the encoder); C06 (physics observer feeding an OCSVM).
- The closest thing to a placement comparison is C19, which runs three physics/learned streams with a calibrated fusion and so may contain stream ablations, and C18's "features over architecture" finding (physics features carry the signal regardless of architecture). Both need full-text checks.

### KQ3: Vehicle applications, real vs simulated data
**Takeaway.** Vehicle-domain physics+ML detectors are almost all evaluated in **simulation or HIL**. None of the screened abstracts applies a physics-informed learned detector to a real passenger car's decoded CAN signals. Within this search, that is the project's novelty space.
- C01: data "synthesised using ... IPG/TruckMaker® integrated with a brake hardware setup" (abstract); heavy commercial vehicle platoon, replay/sinusoidal/freeze attacks.
- C02: "validated in an OPAL-RT hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulation testbed" (abstract).
- C03: "as shown through the simulation studies" (abstract).
- C05 and C06: the abstracts say "numerical experiments" and do not name the dataset (see section 5). C07 (same group, physics-only) used "real-world connected vehicle data" (abstract).
- C08: attack model "created from real vehicle data" (abstract); results "preliminary".
- C11: RC car testbed plus drone HIL (abstract), so a real small-scale ground vehicle.
- C09 and C10: VeReMi / VeReMi Extension (simulated V2X); C10 states "'realistic' denotes a noise-inclusive simulation condition rather than real-world validation" (abstract).

### Ranked shortlist (most useful for THIS project)
1. **C01 Philip et al. 2026 (IEEE ISI)** covers RQ1 and is the closest prior work. Same-family comparison (CNN vs LSTM vs PI-LSTM) using physics residuals from longitudinal vehicle dynamics, with replay, sinusoidal and freeze attacks that the project can mirror. Key numbers: "AUROC of 98.33% and F1 score of 95.74%" (abstract). Limitation the project addresses: platoon V2V states from TruckMaker plus brake HIL, not real-car CAN. The LSTM-only F1 (93.86%) still needs a full-text page locator.
2. **C18 Chahine & Noura 2026 (Future Internet)** covers RQ1, RQ2 and D3. It is the cleanest **same-architecture** test of physics features vs raw inputs on real testbed data: "PA-F1 = 0.7343 for physics features versus 0.6687 for raw [SCADA]" with USAD (an autoencoder-family detector); a GRU refinement stage helped on one dataset (+8.1 pp) and hurt on the other (−6.8 pp) (abstract). This is a template for the D3 vs no-physics ablation, and a warning that temporal models can hurt on brief perturbations.
3. **C16/C17 Zideh & Solanki 2024/2025 (IEEE PESGM; SEGAN)** cover RQ1 and D4. A convolutional AE with a physical-law term in the loss vs the same AE without it. PESGM abstract: "more accurate results compared to the data-driven ConvAE model ... less training time to converge"; the SEGAN extension adds a real feeder and data-scarcity tests (abstract). This is the most-cited physics-loss AE detector found and the methodological model for D4 (AE + lambda x physics loss).
4. **C05 van Wyk et al. 2020 (IEEE T-ITS)** covers RQ1 and RQ3. The highest-impact vehicle paper: CNN + Kalman/chi-square hybrid that "outperforms ... both CNNs and Kalman filtering with a χ2-detector method alone" (abstract). It supports the three-way design (physics-only vs learned-only vs hybrid) for automated-vehicle sensor anomalies (speed, acceleration, GPS-type signals).
5. **C19 PIWAD 2026 (IEEE IoT-J)** covers RQ2 (score fusion), D3 and RQ4. It learns physics invariants (mass conservation) as residuals, combines a physics-augmented AE with a physics-violation score through calibrated late fusion, and includes "residual-aware adaptive attackers" (abstract). Results: F1 0.928 / AUC 0.972 (BATADAL) and F1 0.868 (SWaT) (abstract). Directly reusable for RQ4 (attacks that respect the physics).
6. **C02 Guo, Ye, Yang 2021 (IEEE TTE)** is the RQ1/D3 precedent in the EV-powertrain domain. Physics-guided features from power-electronics and vehicle models feed an ML classifier (HIL). The abstract gives no numbers ("high accuracy"). It is an established, cited precedent for physics as inputs in vehicles. C11 AVMON is a close runner-up for RQ3 (hybrid physics + ML residual on a real RC car).
